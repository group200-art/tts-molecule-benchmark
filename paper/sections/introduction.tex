\section{Introduction}
We study how published test-time scaling (TTS) methods transfer to multi-property molecular optimization. \gellmo{} provides instruction-tuned models, molecular editing tasks, and property evaluators \citep{gellmo2025}. We use this interface to compare proposal sampling, refinement, structured search, voting, and budget forcing on fixed checkpoints.

The original \gellmo{} evaluation measures success among twenty candidates. Transferred methods expose different outputs: candidate pools, refinement endpoints, or selected answers. We standardize the evaluated output to one molecule while retaining each method's generation and selection rules.

The benchmark covers nine strategies, two models, ten tasks, and all 3,905 task--input pairs per configuration. We specify the molecular adaptations and report task success (Table~\ref{tab:effectiveness}), generation workload (Table~\ref{tab:workload}), and molecular quality (Table~\ref{tab:quality}). Analyses of cached candidate pools quantify the effect of output selection; minimum-improvement thresholds reveal a different ordering of Sampling and Graph of Thoughts.
